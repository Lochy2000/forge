# Innovate UK Smart Grants — Scoring Criteria and Assessor Guidance

Source: Based on published Innovate UK assessor guidance and competition briefs.
Note: Specific word limits and scoring weights vary by competition — always check the current competition brief.

---

## How applications are assessed

Innovate UK uses independent expert assessors who score each section against published criteria. Applications are ranked by total score. Only applications above the funding threshold are considered, and within that pool, highest scores win funding.

Assessors are typically a mix of technical experts and commercial reviewers. They may not be specialists in your exact field — write for an intelligent non-expert.

Each section is scored on a scale. The most common approach is:
- **0** — Not addressed or not credible
- **1** — Poor — significant weaknesses
- **2** — Moderate — some strengths but notable gaps
- **3** — Good — addresses requirements with minor gaps
- **4** — Excellent — fully addresses requirements with strong evidence

Scores of 0 or 1 on any section typically result in rejection regardless of other scores.

## Section 1: Innovation

### What assessors are looking for

**The core question:** Is this genuinely new, and does the applicant understand what currently exists?

Strong responses:
- State clearly what the current state of the art is — name specific existing solutions, technologies, or approaches
- Explain precisely why the current state of the art is inadequate for the problem
- Describe the specific advance your project makes beyond that baseline
- Quantify the improvement wherever possible (cost, performance, efficiency, speed)
- State the starting TRL and target TRL with justification for both
- Reference academic or industry sources for state of the art claims

Weak responses (score 0–1):
- Vague claims: "our technology is innovative" without specifics
- No comparison to existing solutions
- Claiming novelty that a basic literature search would disprove
- TRL not stated or implausible progression claimed

**The assessor test:** Could a competitor read this section and understand exactly what makes this different from what already exists?

## Section 2: Market Opportunity

### What assessors are looking for

**The core question:** Is there a real, sizeable market that needs this solution, and can this team reach it?

Strong responses:
- Define the specific problem the market has — not "energy storage is important" but "long-duration storage above 8 hours is unavailable at under £200/kWh"
- Size the addressable market with a credible source and date — not just total market size but the specific segment you can reach
- Describe route to market in concrete stages — who buys first, how you reach them, what partnerships exist
- Show awareness of competitors and articulate your differentiation clearly
- Reference real customer validation where possible — letters of intent, pilot agreements, customer conversations

Weak responses:
- Global market size figures without UK or addressable segment breakdown
- No route to market — just "we will sell to industry"
- No competitor analysis or dismissing competitors without evidence
- Market sizing without a source

## Section 3: Team Capability

### What assessors are looking for

**The core question:** Does this specific team have what it takes to deliver this specific project?

Strong responses:
- Name key team members with specific relevant credentials — "PhD in electrochemistry, 8 years industry experience" not "experienced scientist"
- Show the team covers all critical disciplines the project requires
- Acknowledge gaps explicitly and explain how they will be addressed (subcontractors, advisors, recruitment)
- Highlight directly relevant past projects or deliverables
- Subcontractors and academic partners: explain why they are needed and what they contribute

Weak responses:
- Generic CVs with no relevance to the specific project
- All technical, no commercial expertise
- Gaps not acknowledged
- No explanation of how the team will work together

---

## Section 4: Environmental and Social Impact

### What assessors are looking for

**The core question:** What are the broader benefits and risks, and has the applicant thought them through honestly?

Strong responses:
- Quantify positive environmental impact where possible — CO2 savings, energy savings, waste reduction
- Connect impact to UK government targets (net zero 2050, sector-specific targets) using published figures
- Acknowledge negative impacts honestly and describe mitigation — assessors know all projects have some impact
- Social impact: jobs created, skills developed, regional benefits
- Do not overstate — assessors are sceptical of claimed impacts without evidence

Weak responses:
- Generic "our project is good for the environment" without specifics
- No acknowledgement of negative impacts
- Impact claims without any quantification or source

---

## Section 5: Need for Public Funding

### What assessors are looking for

**The core question:** Why specifically can this project not proceed without public funding?

Strong responses:
- Explain concretely why self-funding is not possible at this stage
- Explain why commercial debt or equity finance is not appropriate — this is usually because the technology risk is too high for private lenders
- Demonstrate additionality — the grant enables something that would otherwise not happen, or would be significantly delayed
- Show the grant represents value for money for the taxpayer
- If the project would happen anyway without the grant, say so and explain why the grant still adds value

Weak responses:
- "We need the money" without explaining why public money specifically
- No explanation of why private finance is unavailable
- No additionality argument

---

## Section 6: Risk Management

### What assessors are looking for

**The core question:** Has the team honestly identified the risks and do they have credible plans to manage them?

Strong responses:
- Identify risks across all categories: technical, commercial, financial, team, regulatory, supply chain
- Rate each risk by likelihood and impact
- Provide specific mitigation for each risk — not "we will manage it" but how
- Show contingency plans for high-impact risks
- Acknowledging risks strengthens applications — assessors know innovation is risky and distrust applications that claim no significant risks

Weak responses:
- Only technical risks listed — no commercial or operational risks
- Generic mitigations not specific to the risk
- No contingency planning
- Claiming no significant risks

## Common reasons for rejection

1. **Out of scope** — project does not meet competition eligibility criteria
2. **Not innovative** — advance beyond state of art not demonstrated
3. **Market not credible** — no clear need or route to market
4. **Team gaps** — critical skills missing with no plan to address
5. **Weak additionality** — unclear why public funding is needed
6. **Vague claims** — assertions without evidence throughout
7. **Word limit exceeded** — automatic disqualification in some competitions
8. **Missing mandatory sections** — ensure all required sections are completed

---

## What good applications look like

- Open each section by directly addressing the assessor's core question
- Use specific numbers wherever possible — vague language scores lower
- Cite sources for market data and state of the art claims
- Write for a non-specialist — avoid unexplained jargon
- Be honest about weaknesses and explain how they are managed
- Keep language formal, concise, and evidence-led
- Never exceed word limits
