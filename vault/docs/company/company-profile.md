## Technology and innovation

**Core technology:**
ThermaSync's TLSC (Thermal Load-Shifting Controller) is a software-hardware system that sits at each node in a distributed heat network — district heating, commercial HVAC, industrial process cooling — and coordinates thermal loads across the network in real time. The system uses a predictive algorithm trained on historical demand patterns, weather forecasts, and grid pricing signals to pre-cool or pre-heat buildings before peak periods, shifting demand away from expensive and carbon-intensive peak grid periods without occupant discomfort.

The hardware component is a retrofit controller unit that connects to existing building management systems (BMS) via BACnet and Modbus protocols. No replacement of existing equipment is required.

**Current TRL:** 4
**Evidence for current TRL:**
Laboratory validation of the predictive algorithm on simulated network data covering 14 building archetypes. Bench testing of the controller hardware with three BMS manufacturers confirmed. No live network deployment yet.

**Key differentiators:**
- Retrofit-first: works with existing infrastructure, no capital replacement needed — unlike competitors requiring new smart meters or new heat pumps
- Network-level optimisation: coordinates across all nodes simultaneously rather than optimising each building independently, capturing demand diversity benefits
- Predictive not reactive: shifts loads 2–6 hours ahead of demand peaks using weather and grid price forecasting, not just responding to real-time signals

**IP position:**

- Know-how: Training dataset of 847,000 hours of thermal demand profiles across 23 building types — not publicly available

## Company values and mission relevant to grant writing

ThermaSync's mission is to decarbonise the UK's heat networks without requiring building owners or network operators to replace working infrastructure. The company's approach is explicitly retrofit-first — recognising that the UK's 30 million existing buildings cannot all be rebuilt before 2050 and that the fastest path to net zero heating requires making existing systems smarter, not replacing them.

This aligns directly with:
- UK Government Heat and Buildings Strategy (2021): prioritises low-disruption decarbonisation pathways
- Innovate UK Net Zero Innovation Portfolio: focus on scalable, commercially viable heat decarbonisation
- National Grid ESO Future Energy Scenarios: demand flexibility identified as critical enabler for high-renewable grid
