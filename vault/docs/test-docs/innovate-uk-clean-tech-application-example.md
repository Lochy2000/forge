## Section 1: Innovation

### What is the innovation?

GreenFlux Energy Ltd is developing a next-generation vanadium redox flow battery (VRFB) system capable of storing 10 MWh of energy at a cost below £150/kWh — a 40% reduction on current commercial systems. The core innovation is a proprietary membrane electrolyte composite that eliminates cross-contamination between vanadium half-cells, extending cycle life beyond 25,000 charge-discharge cycles without capacity degradation.

Current state-of-the-art vanadium flow batteries suffer from membrane degradation after 10,000–15,000 cycles, requiring expensive electrolyte rebalancing. Our composite membrane addresses this through a patented polymer crosslinking process that prevents vanadium ion migration while maintaining high proton conductivity.

The project will advance the technology from TRL 4 (laboratory prototype validated) to TRL 7 (prototype demonstrated in operational environment).

### Why is this beyond the current state of the art?

Existing commercial VRFB systems (Sumitomo, Invinity, CellCube) use Nafion-based membranes with known degradation pathways. Academic literature (Zhang et al., 2023; Chen & Williams, 2022) has identified the ion crossover problem but no commercial solution exists at scale. Our approach differs by combining sulfonated polyether ether ketone (SPEEK) with a graphene oxide barrier layer — a combination not previously demonstrated in a commercial VRFB format.

Freedom to operate: Patent search completed November 2023. No blocking patents identified for the specific SPEEK-graphene oxide composite configuration. Patent application GB2401234 filed January 2024.

## Section 2: Market Opportunity

### Market need

The UK grid-scale energy storage market is projected to reach 24 GW of installed capacity by 2035 (National Grid ESO, 2023), driven by the intermittent nature of offshore wind and solar generation. Current lithium-ion battery systems dominate short-duration storage (1–4 hours) but are unsuitable for long-duration storage (8–12 hours) due to thermal runaway risk, degradation at depth of discharge, and end-of-life disposal challenges.

Flow batteries are technically superior for long-duration storage but have been excluded from most UK projects due to capital cost. Our £150/kWh target removes this barrier and opens a serviceable addressable market of £4.2 billion in the UK alone by 2030.

### Route to market

Phase 1 (this project): Demonstrate a 500 kWh pilot system at the University of Birmingham Energy Research Accelerator, generating performance data for grid operator approval.

Phase 2 (post-project): Commercial partnership with a major UK DNO (heads of terms agreed with Western Power Distribution) for a 5 MWh demonstration project at a wind farm connection point in North Yorkshire.

Phase 3: License manufacturing to an established UK battery manufacturer. GreenFlux retains IP ownership and provides technical support under a royalty agreement.

### Competitors and differentiation

| Competitor | Technology | Cost/kWh | Cycle Life |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invinity Energy Systems | Vanadium flow | £220–£260 | 10,000+ |
| CellCube | Vanadium flow | £230–£280 | 10,000+ |
| GreenFlux (target) | VRFB + composite membrane | £140–£160 | 25,000+ |
| Tesla Megapack | Lithium-ion | £180–£220 | 3,000–4,000 |

Our cost advantage comes from reduced electrolyte volume (15% reduction due to higher energy density) and extended operational life removing rebalancing costs.

## Section 3: Environmental Impact and Benefits

### Environmental benefits

This project directly supports the UK's net zero 2050 target and Innovate UK's clean growth priority by enabling greater penetration of renewable energy on the grid.

**Quantified environmental benefits:**

Each 10 MWh GreenFlux system deployed avoids the need for equivalent gas peaker plant capacity. Based on BEIS carbon intensity data (2023), displacing 10 MWh of daily gas peaking generation reduces CO2 emissions by approximately 1,850 tonnes per year per installation.

Projected UK deployment of 200 systems by 2035 would avoid 370,000 tonnes of CO2 per year — equivalent to removing 80,000 petrol cars from UK roads.

**Materials and circular economy:**

Vanadium electrolyte is 100% recyclable and retains its value indefinitely. Unlike lithium-ion batteries, there is no degradation of the active material — vanadium ions do not plate, intercalate, or form dendritic structures. At end of life, the electrolyte is reclaimed and sold back to vanadium suppliers.

The system contains no cobalt, lithium, or other conflict minerals. Steel tank construction uses standard structural grades with established recycling pathways.

**Negative impacts and mitigation:**

Vanadium pentoxide used in electrolyte production is classified as toxic if inhaled during manufacturing. GreenFlux mitigates this through closed-loop electrolyte preparation using automated mixing equipment with local exhaust ventilation, in compliance with COSHH regulations. Operational systems are fully sealed with no human contact with electrolyte during normal operation.

Sulphuric acid is used as the electrolyte solvent. Containment is provided by double-walled steel tanks with secondary containment bunds designed to BS EN 1825 standards. Any spill is fully contained and neutralised on site.

### Social and economic benefits

The project creates 12 direct skilled manufacturing jobs in the West Midlands — a region identified under the Levelling Up agenda — with a further 40 indirect jobs in the supply chain. The University of Birmingham partnership supports 3 PhD studentships in electrochemical engineering.

Grid stabilisation benefits are diffuse but significant: long-duration storage reduces frequency of constraint payments to wind farms (currently costing UK consumers £900m/year) and reduces curtailment of renewable generation.

## Section 5: Project Plan and Work Packages

### Work Package 1: Membrane Scale-Up (Months 1–6)
Scale composite membrane synthesis from 100cm² samples to 1m² sheets suitable for stack assembly. Key milestone: 10 membranes meeting target proton conductivity (>80 mS/cm) and vanadium crossover (<2 mmol/day) specifications.

### Work Package 2: Stack Assembly and Testing (Months 4–10)
Assemble 50 kW pilot stack using scaled membranes. Conduct 1,000 charge-discharge cycles to validate performance stability. Key milestone: capacity retention >95% after 1,000 cycles.

### Work Package 3: System Integration (Months 8–14)
Integrate 10 x 50 kW stacks with BMS, thermal management, and power electronics to form 500 kW/500 kWh pilot system. Commission at University of Birmingham site. Key milestone: system online and accepting grid commands.

### Work Package 4: Grid Validation (Months 12–18)
Operate pilot system under realistic grid conditions including frequency response, peak shaving, and arbitrage cycles. Generate performance dataset for DNO approval process. Key milestone: dataset accepted by Western Power Distribution technical team.

## Section 6: Risk Mitigation

### Technical risks

**Risk: Membrane scale-up fails to meet conductivity targets**
Probability: Medium. Impact: High.
Mitigation: Two alternative membrane formulations identified in laboratory. If primary formulation fails at scale, WP1 has a 6-week contingency to evaluate alternative B (SPEEK with titanium dioxide nanoparticles instead of graphene oxide). Alternative B has achieved 75 mS/cm at laboratory scale — below target but sufficient for project continuation.

**Risk: Vanadium electrolyte supply disruption**
Probability: Low. Impact: Medium.
Mitigation: Three electrolyte suppliers identified (Bushveld Minerals UK, Largo Resources, EVRAZ). Frame agreements in place with two suppliers. Electrolyte stockpile for 6 months operation held at Birmingham site from month 10.

### Commercial risks

**Risk: DNO approval process delayed beyond project timeline**
Probability: Medium. Impact: Medium.
Mitigation: Approval process initiated in parallel with WP4, not dependent on project completion. Western Power Distribution has assigned a dedicated technical liaison. Delay does not affect Innovate UK deliverables — grid validation data generation is the project deliverable, not DNO approval.

**Risk: Competitor product reaches market before GreenFlux commercialises**
Probability: Low. Impact: Medium.
Mitigation: Patent protection filed. 18-month technology lead estimated based on competitor roadmaps. Phase 2 commercial deployment agreement with WPD locks in first-mover advantage in that market segment.

## Section 7: Need for Public Funding

This project cannot be self-funded. GreenFlux raised £850,000 in seed funding in 2022 which has funded the laboratory research to TRL 4. Scale-up from TRL 4 to TRL 7 requires pilot-scale manufacturing equipment (electrolyser, coating line, stack assembly jig) totalling £1.2m capital expenditure that is beyond the company's current means.

Commercial debt finance is not appropriate at this stage. The technology has not yet been validated at scale, making lenders unwilling to provide asset-backed lending against unproven prototype equipment. Asset finance for specialist electrochemical manufacturing equipment does not exist in the UK market.

Without Innovate UK funding, the project would be delayed by a minimum of 3 years while GreenFlux pursues an equity funding round — by which time the grid storage market may have committed to alternative technologies, eliminating the commercial opportunity.

The grant represents additionality: it enables the company to execute a scale-up that would otherwise be impossible at this stage of development, accelerating commercialisation by at least 3 years and securing the UK's position in a high-value clean energy technology.
