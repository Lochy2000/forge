## Policy and regulatory context

- Heat in buildings is 37% of UK emissions (468 MtCO2e total): space heating 17%, industrial heat 14%, hot water 4%, cooking 2%. Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-heat-networks-market-overview/uk-heat-networks-market-overview-accessible-webpage (2024).
- The Heat Networks (Market Framework) (Great Britain) Regulations 2025 establish Ofgem, Citizens Advice, Consumer Scotland and the Energy Ombudsman in the heat network market — regulating heat networks as a utility for the first time. Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2025/9780348266474 (2025).
- Heat Network Zoning, introduced under the Energy Act 2023 and launched in 2025, designates zones where heat networks are the lowest-cost low-carbon heating option. Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/heat-network-zoning (2025).
- Clean Flexibility Roadmap published 23 July 2025 by DESNZ jointly with NESO and Ofgem, setting flexibility milestones to Clean Power 2030 and net zero by 2050. NESO will set a public non-domestic flexibility capacity target by end-December 2025. Source: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68874ddeb0e1dfe5b5f0e431/clean-flexibility-roadmap.pdf (July 2025); landing page https://www.neso.energy/industry-information/flexibility/clean-flexibility-roadmap.
- NESO Demand Flexibility Service (DFS) became all-year-round in November 2024. By March 2025: 28 providers, 1.98 million MPANs registered. Winter 2024/25: 10,983.5 MWh bids, 5,449.6 MWh accepted, £1.22m in accepted tenders. Source: https://www.neso.energy/industry-information/balancing-services/demand-flexibility-service-dfs (2025).

## State of the art

- Independent academic literature shows advanced demand management with 2–3 hour ahead thermal demand forecasting can deliver an average 25% reduction in the thermal load of individual buildings. Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335624058_Demand_side_management_in_district_heating_systems_by_innovative_control (2019).
- UK-specific peer-reviewed work: shifting hot-water heat-pump generation via heat storage could cut peak GB electricity demand by ~7 GW (5%); adding 20% space-heating shifting brings a further 10% reduction. Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378778821007143 (2021).
- Incumbent UK BMS suppliers (Honeywell, Johnson Controls, Schneider, Siemens, Trend, Priva, ABB, Tridium and others) focus on building-level optimisation; cross-network coordination of thermal load is not their standard offering. Source: https://www.carbontrust.com/resources/digital-technologies-for-energy-management-a-buyers-guide/dtem-selecting-the-right-system (2024); https://etl.energysecurity.gov.uk/products/energy-monitoring/building-energy-management-systems (ongoing).
