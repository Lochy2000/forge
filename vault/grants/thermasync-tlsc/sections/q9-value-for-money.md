## Why ThermaSync cannot self-fund this project

ThermaSync has raised £395,000 to date (£150,000 Innovate UK Sustainable Innovation Fund 2022, £200,000 angel 2023, £45,000 UKRI Smart Energy Systems research grant 2023) and earned £47,000 in feasibility-study revenue. Cash position is approximately 9 months runway at current burn. The 18-month TRL 4 → TRL 7 scale-up is a **£400,000 single-applicant project at the 45% experimental-development rate**, requiring a £220,000 company contribution. This is met through stated co-funding capacity (**£180,000** cash reserves) plus **£40,000 from two named commitments already on record**: £25,000 from the Sheffield City Council pilot agreement (Phase 1 feasibility extension, Q3 2024; cashflow Q2 2025) and £15,000 from Applied Electronics Ltd co-investment in shared tooling under the WP3 development agreement. Without grant support, £180,000 of co-funding capacity cannot meet the £220,000 contribution required, and the named pilot and subcontract commitments cannot be activated at the project scale that triggers them.

## Why commercial finance is not appropriate

**Debt finance:** A pre-TRL 7 control-systems software-and-hardware product with one paid pilot is not bankable. Retrofit BMS controllers offer no asset-backed security, and the algorithm has not yet been validated at the network scale UK lenders require.

**Equity:** A pre-Series-A round at this stage would dilute founders heavily before the value-creating TRL 7 milestone. Equity is planned for Phase 2 commercial deployment post-project, contingent on TRL 7 validation; the grant specifically de-risks the TRL 4 → TRL 7 step that makes that raise possible at a sustainable valuation.

**Asset finance:** Specialist BMS retrofit controller manufacturing through Applied Electronics Ltd has no established asset-finance market in the UK.

## Additionality

Without this grant:

- The TRL 4 → TRL 7 transition would be delayed by at least 24 months while ThermaSync pursued an equity round at unfavourable dilution.
- The Sheffield 240-dwelling deployment (WP4) would not proceed at the required scale or timeline.
- The Brindleyplace LOI (conditional on TRL 7 validation) would lapse, ceding the UK heat-network controls opportunity to incumbent BMS vendors (Honeywell, Johnson Controls, Schneider, Siemens, Trend, Priva, ABB, Tridium) whose product roadmaps do not currently address cross-network thermal coordination.
- The two direct project hires (Senior Engineer, Commercial Deployment Lead) would not occur in 2025.

## Value for money

The £180,000 public investment delivers:

- **2 direct skilled engineering jobs** in 18 months, ramping to 8–10 by month 30 (end of the 12-month post-project Phase 2 window, per Q7), located in Sheffield (Levelling Up region).
- **Sector decarbonisation evidence:** a validated 23%-target peak-demand reduction algorithm deployable across ~800 UK district heating networks under the Heat Networks (Market Framework) Regulations 2025.
- **UK-retained IP:** GB2024/TH0012 patent application; an 847,000-hour training dataset of thermal demand profiles held as trade secret.
- **Knowledge spillover:** one open-methodology publication (precedent: ThermaSync's 2022 Sustainable Innovation Fund deliverable in *Energy and Buildings*).
- **Private leverage:** every £1 of grant unlocks £1.22 of private contribution and conditions the planned Phase 2 equity raise.
- **Market gap addressed:** an evidence base for a UK addressable market of £340m (ThermaSync internal market analysis, Frontier Economics 2023) currently unserved by incumbent BMS vendors at the network-coordination layer.
