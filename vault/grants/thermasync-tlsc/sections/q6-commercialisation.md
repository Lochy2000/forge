## Market sizing

The UK district heating market is **USD 1.54 billion in 2025**, growing at 3.19% CAGR to USD 1.86 billion by 2031 (Mordor Intelligence / Research and Markets, 2025, cross-referenced) — approximately **£1.2 billion** in sterling. Wider UK heat-network investment potential is estimated at **£60–80 billion by 2050** (DESNZ, *UK heat networks: market overview*, 2024). Within this, ThermaSync's internal market analysis (citing Frontier Economics, *Heat Network Market Report*, 2023) identifies a **£340 million-per-year UK serviceable addressable market by 2028** for network-level thermal control — the wedge that retrofit controllers can address across district heating, commercial portfolios, and process cooling.

## Customer segments and initial entry

ThermaSync targets three segments:

1. **UK district heating network operators** (~800 networks today, growing under Heat Network Zoning). Anchor: Sheffield City Council — paid pilot signed, 240-dwelling network.
2. **Commercial property portfolio managers** with 20+ buildings, where demand diversity is exploitable. Anchor: Brindleyplace Estate, Birmingham — LOI signed, 6-building portfolio, contingent on TRL 7.
3. **Industrial site operators** with process heating and cooling loads above 500 kW.

Post-project entry concentrates on segments 1 and 2. The first wave of Heat Network Zoning designations (2025–2027 rollout) creates a target set of named district networks where TLSC's first-mover position with operators is most valuable.

## Route to market

- **Phase 1 (this project, 18 months):** TRL 7 demonstration at Sheffield and Brindleyplace. A successful TRL 7 sign-off at the Sheffield 240-dwelling network triggers the **conversion clause in the Sheffield City Council pilot agreement**, positioning ThermaSync as the incumbent controller supplier for the full zoning-designated network rollout across the **S2 and S3 postcodes**.
- **Phase 2 (post-project, 12 months):** Five paid commercial deployments via the BMS-retrofit subcontractor route, targeting GHNF/HNIP-funded networks where over £1 billion of public capex (HNIP £320m, GHNF £753m, HNES £77m, HNDU £37m — DESNZ 2024) is already committed.
- **Phase 3 (24+ months):** Recurring revenue at scale through a per-node hardware + SaaS analytics licence; channel partnerships with the BMS integrators named in Q4.

## Competitive position and differentiation

Eight named BMS incumbents dominate the UK market (Honeywell, JCI, Schneider, Siemens, Trend, Priva, ABB, Tridium — Carbon Trust *DTEM Buyer's Guide*, 2024). None offer cross-network thermal coordination as a standard product. The patent application (**GB2024/TH0012**) and the 847,000-hour training dataset (held as trade secret) protect a 12–18 month technology lead.

## Business capability

Commercial lead: **Sarah Chen** — MBA Warwick, 8 years in energy consultancy at Atkins and WSP delivering 14 UK district heating feasibility studies; contributing author, CIBSE *Heat Networks Code of Practice* (2021).
