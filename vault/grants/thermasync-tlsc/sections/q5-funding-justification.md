# Q5 — Justification for funding

## Why ThermaSync cannot self-fund this project

ThermaSync is an early-revenue SME: founded 2021, £47,000 turnover in 2023, approximately nine months runway at current burn. Existing cash funds the six-person team and the Sheffield City Council pilot at its current feasibility scope. The company's stated cash co-funding capacity is £180,000. Combined with £40,000 from two named commitments already on record — £25,000 from the Sheffield pilot agreement (Phase 1 feasibility extension, Q3 2024) and £15,000 from Applied Electronics Ltd co-investment in shared WP3 tooling, itemised at Q9 — that brings total contribution capacity to £220,000, exactly matching the company share of a £400,000 single-applicant project at the 45% experimental-development rate. Without the grant, ThermaSync cannot absorb the £180,000 of eligible cost the public award would cover, and the TRL 4 → TRL 7 transition cannot be financed from internal cash.

## Why commercial finance is not appropriate

TLSC is pre-TRL 7 with no live network deployment. **Asset-backed lending is not available** against prototype controller hardware that has not been validated outside the laboratory — UK lenders do not lend against unvalidated R&D equipment. **Equity finance is technically available** — ThermaSync raised a £200,000 angel round in 2023 — but a further round at this stage would take 18–24 months to close, dilute the founder team below strategically useful levels, and **push the project past the regulatory window** opened by the Heat Networks (Market Framework) Regulations 2025 and Heat Network Zoning, when first-mover position with named district-network operators is most valuable.

## Match between ThermaSync's stage and Smart Grants

Smart Grants are designed for SME experimental development across the TRL 4 → TRL 7 range this project covers, and require clear start- and end-of-project TRL statements — a strong fit for the 45% experimental-development funding rate. ThermaSync has followed the Innovate UK funding pathway as intended: a **£150,000 Innovate UK Sustainable Innovation Fund award (2022)** delivered the algorithm proof of concept; a **£45,000 UKRI Smart Energy Systems award (2023)** validated the 48-hour demand-forecasting model (4.2% MAPE across 14 archetypes, beating the industry-standard persistence model by 31%). Smart Grants is the right next step to integrate those validated components into a deployable network-level controller. Prior grant win rate: 2 of 3.

## Counterfactual — what happens without funding

Without this grant the Sheffield pilot remains a feasibility study, the Brindleyplace letter of intent (contingent on TRL 7 validation) lapses, and TLSC deployment is delayed by an estimated **24–30 months** while ThermaSync pursues equity finance — by which time named operators inside the first Heat Network Zoning designations will have committed to alternative controllers. The grant delivers genuine additionality: UK-market entry in 2026–2027 rather than 2028–2029, securing UK-developed technology in a newly-regulated utility market that would otherwise default to the global BMS incumbents.
